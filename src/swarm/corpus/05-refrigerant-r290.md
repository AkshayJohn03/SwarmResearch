# Working with R-290 Propane Refrigerant

R-290 is high-purity propane used as the refrigerant in every K-Series heat pump. This note summarises the handling rationale and the safety envelope that installers and service partners must respect.

Why propane. R-290 has a global warming potential of 3 over 100 years, effectively negligible compared with R-410A at over 2,000. It is a single-component fluid, so recharge after a leak does not fractionate, and it is widely available without the quota allocation problems that affected blended refrigerants in recent years. Thermodynamically, R-290 supports high condensing efficiency, which is one reason the K-Series reaches its rated seasonal efficiency figures.

Safety envelope. Propane is flammable, so charge sizes are limited by room-area rules in the relevant EN standard. The maximum permitted charge in a K-Series outdoor unit is below the threshold that would require permanent leak detection in a typical detached-house installation. Copper and brass components must be brazed with ventilation; the service manual prohibits open flames within one metre of a opened circuit, and partners must use nitrogen purging during any soldering work on an R-290 circuit.

Handling rules for partners include certified training recertification every three years, use of spark-free recovery machines, and transport in approved cylinders. The refrigerant label on the outdoor unit must remain legible; a missing label is treated as a warranty-relevant defect at commissioning inspection.

Recycling. End-of-life units are recovered through the take-back programme described in the recycling documentation, where the R-290 charge is recovered, filtered, and re-refined for reuse in industrial refrigeration. Recovery yield targets in the programme handbook are above 95 percent of nameplate charge.
