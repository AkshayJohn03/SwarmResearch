# Field Failure Analysis 2024-2025

Reliability engineering report covering warranty claims and telemetry data from the connected fleet between January 2024 and June 2025.

Overall rates. The fleet-level failure rate for the K-Series stands at 0.8 percent per year across all modules, measured as claims per installed unit-year. This is an improvement from 1.3 percent in the 2023 cohort, which reliability engineering attributes mainly to the hydraulic stability guidance introduced with the installation guidelines and to the retuned modulation controller that reduced short-cycling.

Failure distribution. The most common single failure mode is the outdoor fan assembly, accounting for roughly a third of claims, typically bearing wear accelerated by coastal salt exposure. Electronic control boards follow, mostly from surges in regions with unstable grids. The compressor module itself shows the lowest claim rate of all major modules; where compressor claims do occur, the health ledger almost always shows anomalous start counts or inverter temperature histograms months in advance, which is the basis of the predictive diagnostic visit.

Water-side issues. Hard-water fouling appears in about one in ten service visits in the affected regions, though the fouling rig data from the heat exchanger design notes shows the plate stack itself stays within tolerance when the magnetic filter is installed as specified.

Actions. Reliability engineering recommends three changes already in qualification: a sealed bearing variant for coastal fan assemblies, surge protection level adjustment for export markets with unstable grids, and automatic diagnostic-visit creation when the compressor health ledger crosses its warning band, rather than waiting for the biennial partner inspection.
